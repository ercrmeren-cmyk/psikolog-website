A 40-second pause

Before you read on, breathe with me

You don’t need a session to start turning down the noise. This is one of the first exercises I teach in therapy: when you lengthen your out-breath, your body understands that it can let go of the alarm.

Drop your shoulders, rest your feet on the floor and follow the circle.

That’s it. You may notice very little, or nothing at all, and that’s fine too.

What matters is that your body knows how to calm down when you give it the right conditions. In therapy, we learn to create them far beyond these 40 seconds.

I’ve never asked for help. I can handle this on my own

💭 Of course you can… you’ve been proving it for a long time. But inside you’re more and more exhausted, more anxious, further away from yourself. And still, something stops you asking for help, because you’re afraid of no longer being the strong one, the one who can handle everything. But look at yourself… this version of you, pushed to the limit, isn’t who you want to be either. You don’t have to break before you allow yourself support. Accepting it doesn’t make you weak; it lets you be yourself again.

It’s not that bad… I can cope

🌿 That’s what you tell yourself while your mind won’t stop, you sleep badly and every day feels heavier. You’ve got used to living like this, but that doesn’t make it normal. Coping isn’t the same as being well… it’s surviving. And deep down you know it: you don’t want this to be your life.

🔑

What if it doesn’t work for me?

✨ The scariest thing isn’t that it might not work… it’s that nothing changes. Carrying on feeling trapped, exhausted and disconnected from yourself, while on the outside everything stays the same. You’ve already tried pushing harder, demanding more of yourself, holding on longer… and it has only brought you here. Maybe it isn’t about being able to do more, but about no longer doing it alone and starting to do it differently.

Everything you may be wondering before taking the first step

The most important thing is that you come willing to explore and with an open mind. In therapy, we’ll work together to identify which situations are causing you distress, which emotional blocks you’re facing and how they’re affecting your daily life. From there, we’ll go step by step to overcome those challenges in a practical way that suits you, with tools to help you recover your emotional well-being.

Therapy doesn’t have to be a long process. It all depends on the situation you’re going through, your goals and how ready you are for change.

In some cases, a single session can bring emotional relief; in others, more frequent or longer-term support is needed. Sometimes you come for a specific issue, we resolve it and you no longer need to continue.

What I can promise is that if at any point we feel the process isn’t moving forward as it should, I’ll offer you other options or refer you to someone better placed to help. Your emotional well-being comes first.

The first session lasts 60 minutes; all following sessions last 55 minutes.

The first session is key: it lets us get to know each other, see whether we’re a good fit and, above all, set clear goals based on what you’re going through. From there, we’ll map out a personalised plan, agreeing the frequency that suits you best (weekly, fortnightly, etc.), as well as the fees and available times.

The whole process is designed so that therapy adapts to your emotional and personal needs.

Therapy is completely voluntary and flexible. You can stop at any time if you choose to.

That said, if at any point you don’t feel comfortable or feel that something isn’t working as you hoped, I encourage you to talk about it openly. Communication is key for therapy to work, and I’m here to listen without judgement.

And if you finally decide not to continue, I’ll help you find other professional options that better suit your needs… if that’s what you’d like.
